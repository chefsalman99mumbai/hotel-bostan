Since 1980, Hotel Bostan has cooked over four fires — a clay tandoor, a charcoal sigri, sealed handis, and a marble pass — in the heart of Jogeshwari West, Mumbai. The kitchen is purely Halal and draws on North Indian, Moghlai, Chinese, Mexican, and Mediterranean traditions. Forty covers are arranged around a single long table and a working pass, so guests watch the last thirty seconds of every plate come together. Long 150 words

Hotel Bostan — a Halal kitchen in Jogeshwari West, Mumbai. Founded in 1980, the restaurant has spent forty-five years cooking over four fires: a clay tandoor at 480°C, a charcoal sigri lit every afternoon at three, sealed clay handis that simmer for four unhurried hours, and a working marble pass where one chef finishes forty covers. The menu moves across North Indian, Moghlai, Chinese, Mexican, and Mediterranean traditions — from slow-cooked Kashmiri classics to wok-tossed noodles and whole burrata torn over heirloom tomato. Everything is cooked to order, over fire, without shortcuts. Produce arrives daily from Nashik and Pune, ghee is hand-churned in Rajasthan, sea salt is sourced from Kutch, and the herbs are grown on the roof, four floors above the dining hall. Open every day, 12:00 – 23:30. Reservations recommended.

The Story

Hotel Bostan — a Halal kitchen in Jogeshwari West, where the fire is old and the welcome older still. Since 1980, everything on the menu has begun within a few hours of this table, and nothing leaves the kitchen until it has met one of four fires.

For over four decades the dining hall has been built around a single long table and a working marble pass, so guests watch the last thirty seconds of every plate come together. Forty covers, one chef at the pass, one kitchen behind glass. It is a room that behaves, quietly, like the fire it cooks over: charcoal, salt, patience — and a welcome that never closes.

It is a kitchen that behaves, quietly, like the fire it cooks over: charcoal, salt, patience — and a welcome that never closes.

The menu moves across five traditions without ever losing the room's single voice. North Indian classics sit beside Awadhi kormas and Bihari seekh. A wok chapter shares the page with whole burrata torn over heirloom tomato. Nothing is fusion for its own sake — every plate has a reason to be here, and every one of them has met a fire.

Nothing is fusion for its own sake — every plate has a reason to be here, and every one of them has met a fire.

Produce arrives daily from Nashik and Pune. Ghee is hand-churned in Rajasthan. Citrus comes from Nagpur, sea salt from Kutch, and the herbs — curry leaf, mint, coriander, bay — are grown on our own roof, four floors above the dining hall.

The Mediterranean chapter at Bostan was born out of our Sunday brunch — one day a week where the fire rests and the table turns toward olive oil, warm bread, slow eggs, and mezze.
