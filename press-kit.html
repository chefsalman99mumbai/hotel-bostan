The idea

Bostan means garden. We took the word as a brief: cook what a garden gives, with fire, salt and patience — and serve it slowly. Since 1980, everything on the menu has begun within a few hours of this table, and nothing leaves the kitchen until it has met one of four fires.

Chef Salman has led the pass for over two decades. The room is built around a single long table and a working marble counter, so guests watch the last thirty seconds of every plate come together. It is a restaurant that behaves, quietly, like a garden: seasonal, unhurried, and entirely dependent on what the morning brought in.

The kitchen

Four fires, one brigade, forty covers a night. Each fire does one thing — and does it every night, without fail.

01

Tandoor

Clay oven at 480°C. Breads, tikkas, and the smoke that gets under everything we serve.

02

Sigri

Charcoal grill lit at three in the afternoon. Seekh, chops, the char you can smell from the street.

03

Handi

Sealed clay pot, slow heat, four hours. Korma, nihari, gravies that taste of patience.

04

The pass

Marble counter, one chef, forty covers. Every plate leaves this surface in under a minute.

A note on sourcing & kitchen

Produce arrives daily from Nashik and Pune. Ghee is hand-churned in Rajasthan. Citrus comes from Nagpur, sea salt from Kutch, and the herbs — curry leaf, mint, coriander, bay — are grown on our own roof, four floors above the dining room. Our kitchen is purely Halal; we do not serve alcohol or pork, and we do not close for Mondays.

The room & the table

Forty covers across one long table and a run of two-tops, all facing a working marble pass. Candlelight, exposed brick, brass, and a barrel-vaulted ceiling that keeps the noise low and the room warm.

The Alcove — twelve seats behind a closing door — is available for private dinners, tastings and press previews. Menus are written to the season and confirmed a week ahead.
